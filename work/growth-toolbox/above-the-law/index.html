How we counted & download the records +

Each bar counts CCC records dated 1 January–31 December 2025, in the 50 US states or Washington, DC, explicitly coded as in-person. There are 39,079 eligible records. Multi-day and recurring actions count on each recorded day; one event can appear in several bars, but only once within a bar. Exact duplicate records are removed.

We searched claims_summary, reviewed the matching demand phrases and excluded opposing or ambiguous claims. These are our keyword-bounded classifications, not CCC’s official movement totals:

  • Epstein: explicit Epstein-file disclosure or accountability demands.
  • Gaza: Gaza/Palestine solidarity, opposition to the war or support for Palestinian rights. Hostage-only and opposing claims excluded.
  • Racial justice: explicit Black Lives Matter, racial-justice, anti-police-brutality or anti-police-violence demands. Broader than BLM-branded events.
  • Abortion: abortion access or reproductive rights, freedom or justice. Anti-abortion and general healthcare-only claims excluded.
  • Climate: explicit climate action or justice, opposition to climate change, denial or fossil fuels, or support for renewable energy.
  • Immigration: immigrant rights and protection, opposition to deportations or ICE/CBP enforcement, raids and detention. Pro-enforcement claims excluded.
  • Ukraine: explicit solidarity, aid or sovereignty demands, or opposition to Russia’s invasion of Ukraine. Anti-aid and ceasefire-only claims excluded.

Limits: coverage and claim detail vary by cause. Keyword rules miss differently worded demands; coalition coding can assign shared demands to many local events. CCC also carries forward scheduled repeats, which may not be individually reconfirmed. These counts are not a complete census, unique campaigns, turnout or a measure of outrage.

Not a conversion rate.

Policy views, movement support and climate concern are different measures. The polls do not identify protesters; event-days do not count people. Recurring events, coalition demands and the breadth of each cause affect the totals. The Epstein poll samples registered voters; the others sample US adults. Being named in a file is not evidence of wrongdoing.

Why these polls? / Download the comparison

We retained six national adult surveys from 2025 with inspectable questions, results and methods. For Epstein, we replaced document-release support with a February 2026 registered-voter question that directly measures demand for more investigations and prosecutions. This improves the question’s relevance but introduces a timing and population mismatch. It is not an exhaustive search or an annual average. Climate concern is an indirect proxy, not measured support for climate action.

Question wording, response scales, sample populations, methods and months differ. A scatterplot, ratio or ranking of “dissatisfaction” would suggest a comparability these sources do not provide. The opinion dots run from 0–100%; the event bars use a separate, fixed 0–14,000 scale. Filtering never rescales either.

Navigator’s 72% measures demand for further action. Reuters/Ipsos’s separate 69% measures a belief that powerful people are rarely held accountable. Neither establishes guilt, support for punishing everyone named in the files, or why the earlier protests occurred.

Navigator / Global Strategy Group surveyed 1,000 US registered voters on 19–23 February 2026 through an opt-in online panel with voter-file verification. The headline is a choice between more investigations/prosecutions and enough accountability/time to move on, with a not-sure option: 72%, 17% and 11%, respectively. The publisher reports ±3.1 percentage points (95%); this does not cover every source of error. Exact wording and methods are in the comparison download and source PDF, pp. 3 and 16.

Separately, Reuters/Ipsos surveyed 1,117 US adults aged 18+ through its probability-based KnowledgePanel. Results are weighted; design effect 1.06. All four Qtk statements are included. Full sampling and weighting details are on pages 1 and 16 of the PDF.

Historical data: all protests and six comparison polls in 2025; both Epstein polls in February 2026. Sources checked 17 September 2026. These separate datasets cannot establish cause and effect. Earlier legal records and protest reports are retained in the project files but no longer form a page section.
