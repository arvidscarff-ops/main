02 / HISTORICAL RESISTANCE · WORLDWIDE

Anger is not an outcome.

Before asking why people do not overthrow power today, look at campaigns that tried. NAVCO records large, organised campaigns seeking regime change, independence or an end to occupation—not every protest.

How often did campaigns succeed?

NAVCO 2.1 · % of completed campaigns achieving a stated maximalist goal · grouped by primary method in the final observed year. Retrospective association, not an experimental effect of choosing a method.

View counts & calculation

Success rate = successful completed campaigns ÷ completed campaigns in the group × 100. One campaign, one observation. Ongoing campaigns are excluded, not treated as failures.

WHAT WOULD SUPPORT THE HYPOTHESIS?

Less participation at higher entertainment use.

A negative association would be consistent with the idea—but could still reflect age, education, income, institutions or other differences.

WHAT WOULD CHALLENGE IT?

More entertainment and more participation together.

A positive association challenges a simple country-level “entertainment equals apathy” story. It still cannot establish what causes what.

04 / THE QUESTION THAT REMAINS

Attention is abundant.
Accountability isn’t automatic.

The compelling story is not “Netflix stopped the revolution.” It is the distance between seeing a problem, acting on it, and achieving change—and what we still need to measure.

OBSERVED

Global reach. Unequal resources.

Internet use expanded; Netflix’s worldwide membership base grew over the period shown; global income remains concentrated. These are context, not an explanation for political outcomes.

TESTABLE HYPOTHESIS

Entertainment could displace action.

It could also help people connect, learn and organise. To distinguish these mechanisms, we need evidence about the same people, their exposure and their behaviour over time.

NOT ESTABLISHED

“Nothing happened.”

This page does not establish a worldwide decline in protest or a lack of response to scandals. Recent offline mobilisation and political consequences are not measured in these charts.

The Epstein files: a case worth testing, not a conclusion
DOCUMENTED EVENT · 30 JANUARY 2026

A release is an event. Its impact is a separate question.

The US Department of Justice reported a cumulative release of nearly 3.5 million pages under the Epstein Files Transparency Act on 30 January 2026. This establishes a public-release milestone, not the size or absence of a public response. DOJ announcement ↗

A person being mentioned in records is not, by itself, evidence of wrongdoing. Document volume is not a measure of guilt or institutional accountability.

What a defensible next study needs

  1. Define one geography, event date and observation window before inspecting results.
  2. Measure attention, protest events and institutional responses separately, with consistent coverage.
  3. Compare with a pre-event baseline and similar events; account for reporting changes and competing news.
  4. Publish uncertainty and counterexamples—not a claim that outrage “should” produce violence.

No attention or protest values have been invented for this case study.

Pre-tax income is not wealth or disposable income. WID harmonises and estimates distributions from multiple sources; historical values can include modelling, interpolation and extrapolation. 03 Netflix annual reports Worldwide memberships · 2019–2024

Consolidated global streaming paid memberships at 31 December, transcribed from the 2021 and 2024 annual reports. The original tables report thousands; dividing by 1,000 gives millions. This is a fixed six-year series, not a claim to show the latest membership disclosure.

Memberships are not people, households measured by a census, screen time, or the whole entertainment market. Netflix’s 2024 definition excludes extra-member subaccounts. Company expansion, product availability and account-sharing policies affect the series, independently of political behaviour.

04 NAVCO 2.1 / Chenoweth & Shay Resistance campaigns · 1945–2013

384 campaigns represented by 2,717 campaign-year rows. The page takes each campaign’s last observed year, excludes 53 coded ongoing campaigns from the outcome comparison, and groups the remaining 331 by final-year prim_meth (0 = primarily violent; 1 = primarily nonviolent). Success is the source’s final-year success = 1. The optional sensitivity filter removes campaigns with both primary methods recorded across years.

This is our explicitly defined aggregation, not a reproduction of a published headline success rate. The static NAVCO 1 designation is not used because it is unknown for 141 campaigns. Period filters use the final observed year. Campaign dates shown refer to years observed in this file.

Coverage is a research consensus list of mature, maximalist campaigns with at least 1,000 participants in at least one year. It excludes routine policy protests and can miss small campaigns crushed early. Outcome coding and selection bias matter. The attached codebook contains some references to a later 2017 endpoint; this data file ends in 2013. No post-2013 outcomes are inferred.

05 Eurostat EU regional comparison · 2022 & 2024

ICT table isoc_ci_ac_i; total individuals, unit PC_IND, ages 16–74. Commercial video-on-demand (I_IUVOD), social networking (I_IUSNET), civic/political activity (I_IUCPP), consultations or voting including petitions (I_IUVOTE), and expression of political opinions online (I_IUPOL2). Activities refer to the previous three months. Categories overlap; they are not mutually exclusive or a time budget.

Optional deprivation measure: EU-SILC ilc_mdsd11, all ages, both sexes, percent. Severe material and social deprivation means being unable to afford at least seven of thirteen specified items. Its population differs from the ICT survey; it is not a direct measure of comfort or contentment.

The explorer includes the 27 current EU countries and uses same-year pairs. Missing pairs are excluded and named; all countries have equal weight in Pearson’s r. No regression adjustment, statistical significance or individual-level inference is implied. Source flags remain in tables and downloads: b = break; e = estimated; p = provisional; u = low reliability; d = differing definition. Consult Eurostat for other codes. Survey uncertainty is not available in this extract.

A note on interpretation

This is an exploratory editorial project, not a causal study. There is no invented “revolution score,” no merged index of outrage, and no assumption that political violence is the necessary response to injustice. The most useful next question is what converts attention into durable collective action.
