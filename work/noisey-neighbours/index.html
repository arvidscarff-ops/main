Stockholm / 2021—2024 / Rave collective

NoiseyNeighbours

A friend and I bought a far too large speaker system for his birthday party. When it was over, we were left with roughly 800 kilos of speakers and no sensible use for them.

We had spent the previous years exploring Stockholm’s rave scene. Starting our own events felt like the obvious answer.

≈800kilos of speakers
looking for a purpose

From one party to a collective

The night was only the visible part.

Noisey Neighbours grew into a multi-year rave collective. A partner and I carried the primary responsibility for turning each idea into a functioning night.

I worked across project management, branding and art direction, organic marketing, paid promotion, finance, locations, purchasing, logistics and team coordination. On the night itself, the work continued through setup, door, bar and wardrobe operations, then teardown when everyone else went home.

The collective also gave us an owned stage for our own music. Instead of waiting for a venue or promoter to make room, we built the room ourselves.

The operating system behind a rave

One night. Five linked systems.

  1. 01

    Concept

    Shape the atmosphere, identity and reason for the night.

  2. 02

    Place

    Find a location, understand its constraints and plan the physical build.

  3. 03

    Signal

    Create the brand, content and distribution that bring the right people in.

  4. 04

    Live

    Coordinate sound, team, door, bar, wardrobe and the audience experience.

  5. 05

    Reset

    Teardown, settle finances, learn from the night and begin again.

What I learned

Designing the experience meant owning the operation.

  • Build community and demand with limited paid distribution.
  • Coordinate interdependent work against a fixed, public deadline.
  • Treat brand, environment, music and service as one audience experience.
  • Create infrastructure when existing venues or gatekeepers do not provide it.
  • Learn from real audience behaviour rather than a plan on paper.

I loved what the nights became. I also learned that I like building systems, but not systems that need several hundred kilos of equipment and a teardown at sunrise.
